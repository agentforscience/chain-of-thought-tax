# The Chain-of-Thought Tax: When Do Reasoning Traces Hurt Rather Than Help?

## 1. Executive Summary

**Question.** Does zero-shot chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting lower accuracy on pattern-matching and
factual-recall tasks by 5-15 points, raise it on logical deduction and arithmetic by 10-25 points, and help
fewer task categories as models get larger?

**Answer.** Mostly no. Across five sizes of Qwen2.5-Instruct (0.5B to 14B parameters), 8 tasks and 2,350
items per model:

- **There was no accuracy tax on pattern matching or factual recall.** The CoT effect ranged from -2.2 to
  +3.8 points across the ten model-by-category cells; none was significantly negative, and none fell in
  the hypothesised -5 to -15 range.
- **CoT helped on logic and arithmetic, but not by the hypothesised amount.** Arithmetic gained 48 to 85
  points (far above the 10-25 range) because answering arithmetic with no visible working is near 0%.
  Logical deduction ranged from -7 points (0.5B) to +29 points (14B).
- **The scale trend ran opposite to the hypothesis.** The number of tasks with a significant CoT benefit
  rose from 2 of 8 (0.5B, 1.5B) to 4 of 8 (7B, 14B). The share of previously-correct answers that CoT
  broke fell with size in every category.

**Practical implication.** In this model family the cost of CoT on "easy" categories is tokens, not
accuracy: about 75x more output tokens for no measurable gain on classification and at most a few points
on recall. The real accuracy risk is at the small end: the 0.5B model lost ground on logical deduction,
largely because its reasoning ran past the token budget without reaching an answer.

Scope: one model family, up to 14B parameters, zero-shot, one main prompt pair. The results do not speak
to frontier-scale API models, which could not be tested (no API key was available in the environment).

## 2. Research Question & Motivation

**Hypothesis as given.** "Chain-of-thought prompting degrades performance on pattern matching and factual
recall tasks by 5-15% while improving logical deduction and arithmetic by 10-25%, with the crossover point
shifting toward fewer beneficial categories as model scale increases, suggesting that larger models
internalize reasoning steps that explicit traces disrupt."

I split it into three testable parts (percentages read as accuracy points; relative changes are in
`results/category_level.csv`):

- **H1**: delta = acc(CoT) - acc(direct) is in [-15, -5] for pattern matching and factual recall.
- **H2**: delta is in [+10, +25] for logical deduction and arithmetic.
- **H3**: fewer categories benefit as parameter count grows.

The final clause (larger models "internalise" reasoning that traces disrupt) is a mechanism claim that
accuracy data cannot test directly; section 5 says what the data are and are not consistent with.

**Why it matters.** CoT is used by default and costs far more output tokens. If it also cost accuracy on
some task types, routing prompts by task type would be worth real engineering effort.

**Literature context.** No literature review was pre-gathered for this run (the resource-finder stage was
skipped), so this context is from memory rather than a fresh systematic review. Wei et al. (2022) and
Kojima et al. (2022) showed CoT gains mainly on arithmetic and symbolic tasks. Sprague et al. (2024) found
in a large meta-analysis that CoT gains concentrate on math and symbolic reasoning, with little benefit
elsewhere. Liu et al. (2024) showed CoT can actively hurt on tasks where deliberation hurts humans, such
as implicit pattern learning. Most such comparisons mix model families, so scale is confounded with
training recipe. The contribution here is a within-family scale ladder with paired item-level statistics
that separate "no gain" from "breaks answers the model otherwise gets right".

## 3. Experimental Setup

### Deviation from the default setup
The instructions called for API models via OpenRouter. `OPENROUTER_KEY` and all other provider keys were
unset, so I ran real open-weight models locally instead. This costs external validity (no frontier models)
but gives a cleaner scale comparison: one family, one training recipe, a 30x parameter range.

### Models
Qwen/Qwen2.5-{0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B}-Instruct, loaded in bfloat16 with Hugging Face `transformers`
5.19.0 / `torch` 2.14.1. Snapshot hashes are in `results/config.json`. Greedy decoding
(`do_sample=False`), system prompt "You are a helpful assistant.", zero-shot.

### Prompt conditions
Each item's text ends with an answer-format hint (e.g. "The answer must be exactly one of: World, Sports,
Business, Sci/Tech."). The condition adds one suffix:

| Condition | Suffix | Assistant prefill | Max new tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | `Give only the final answer, with no explanation or reasoning. Respond in the format "Answer: <answer>".` | `Answer:` | 24 |
| CoT | `Think step by step and write out your reasoning. Then finish with a final line in the format "Answer: <answer>".` | none | 1024 |
| CoT-v2 (robustness) | `Before answering, carefully work through the problem and explain your thought process. When you are done, end your response with one last line of the form "Answer: <answer>".` | none | 1024 |

The prefill in the direct condition forces the model to answer immediately, so "direct" really means no
visible reasoning. Exact templates are in `src/scoring.py`.

### Tasks
300 items per task sampled with seed 42 (BBH has only 250; all are used). Total 2,350 items.

| Category | Task | Source | Answer format | Chance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern matching | AG News topic | fancyzhx/ag_news test | 4 labels | 25% |
| Pattern matching | SST-2 sentiment | stanfordnlp/sst2 validation | 2 labels | 50% |
| Factual recall | TriviaQA | rc.nocontext validation | free-form, alias match | ~0% |
| Factual recall | MMLU, 7 recall-heavy subjects | cais/mmlu test | 4-way multiple choice | 25% |
| Logical deduction | BBH logical_deduction_five_objects | lukaemon/bbh | 5-way multiple choice | 20% |
| Logical deduction | ProofWriter, open-world, depth-5 theories | tasksource/proofwriter validation | True / False / Unknown (balanced) | 33% |
| Arithmetic | GSM8K | openai/gsm8k test | number | ~0% |
| Arithmetic | Synthetic 3-operation expressions | generated here | integer | ~0% |

The synthetic arithmetic items, e.g. `(125 + 859) * 4 - 93`, were generated for this study, so they cannot
be in any training set. The MMLU subjects are miscellaneous, world_religions, high_school_geography,
prehistory, anatomy, nutrition and astronomy.

### Scoring
The answer is the text after the last "Answer:" / "answer is" marker (first line of the direct output).
Labels must match exactly one allowed label; multiple choice needs the right letter; numbers are compared
numerically; TriviaQA uses standard normalisation and alias containment. A CoT response with no parsable
answer counts as wrong. Analysis re-scores everything from the saved raw outputs.

I audited extraction by reading samples of wrong-scored CoT answers per task (`src/audit_extraction.py`).
That found one artefact, which is fixed in the reported numbers: the 0.5B model sometimes wrote
"Science/Tech" for the "Sci/Tech" label (it affected both conditions for that model only). In the other
samples the model's stated answer was genuinely wrong.

### Statistics (fixed in `planning.md` before the runs)
- Paired delta per model x task and per model x category (macro-average of the two task deltas), with a
  95% bootstrap CI over items (10,000 resamples, stratified by task).
- Exact McNemar test, Holm-corrected within the 40 task-level tests and within the 20 category-level tests,
  alpha = 0.05.
- H1/H2 verdict per cell: point estimate inside the stated range and Holm-significant.
- H3: slope of the category delta on log2(parameters), CI from the item bootstrap; count of significantly
  helped tasks per model.
- Added metrics: **harm rate** P(CoT wrong | direct right) and **fix rate** P(CoT right | direct wrong).

### Compute and cost
4x NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48 GB each), shared with other jobs during the run. Batch sizes 64-96, reduced
automatically on out-of-memory errors. Inference took about 1 h 50 min wall-clock for the main grid under
contention (the 14B CoT pass alone took about 55 min). API cost: $0. Mixed precision: bfloat16 weights.

### 4.5 Output diagnostics and token cost

Mean output tokens per item were 3.1-3.3 for direct and 222-271 for CoT, a ratio of 71x to 87x
(`results/token_cost.csv`).

CoT responses carried an explicit final-answer marker and finished within budget in at least 95% of
cases for every model-task pair, with two exceptions on the smallest models. On synthetic arithmetic the
0.5B model omitted the marker in 24% of responses (it ended with a boxed number, which the fallback parser
reads). On ProofWriter the small models often ran out of budget. Share of ProofWriter CoT responses that hit the
1,024-token budget without finishing: 44.0% (0.5B), 10.7% (1.5B), 5.0% (3B), 0.3% (7B), 0% (14B).

**Sensitivity analysis.** Restricting to items whose CoT response was well-formed changes one cell
materially: logical deduction at 0.5B moves from -6.9 to +3.7 (n = 407 of 550, CI [-3.8, +11.1]). All
other cells move by less than 2 points (`results/sensitivity_clean_cot.csv`). This subset is selected on
the outcome of the CoT run, so it shows where the loss comes from; it is not an unbiased estimate of
what CoT would do with a larger budget.

### 4.6 Prompt robustness (second CoT wording)

On the first 100 items of each task, the paraphrased CoT instruction gave the same picture
(`results/robustness_cot2.csv`). Across the 19 available model-by-category cells, the correlation between
the two wordings' deltas was 0.996. Signs agreed in 16 of 19 cells; the three disagreements were all cells
where both deltas were within 4 points of zero.

This check is incomplete for the 14B model: its CoT-v2 run was repeatedly killed by out-of-memory errors

One thing to flag: with wording 2, pattern matching shows small negative deltas whose CIs exclude zero for
three models (0.5B: -2.5 [-5.0, -0.5]; 1.5B: -2.5 [-5.0, -0.5]; 14B: -2.3 [-4.7, -0.5]) and a positive one
for 3B (+3.5 [+0.5, +7.0]). These are uncorrected, on 200 or fewer items, and mixed in sign, so I read
them as an upper bound on any classification penalty of roughly 2-5 points, not as evidence for one.

## 5. Analysis & Discussion

### H1 (CoT costs 5-15 points on pattern matching and factual recall): not supported
None of the ten cells is in the range and none is significantly negative. For pattern matching the lower
CI bound is no worse than -3.2 at any size, so a 5-point tax is excluded for the main prompt. For factual
recall a 5-point tax is excluded from 1.5B up; at 0.5B the CI [-5.8, +1.5] just reaches it. At 7B and 14B
recall moves slightly in CoT's favour (+3.8 and +2.8, uncorrected CIs exclude zero, not significant after
Holm correction), driven by TriviaQA.

The closest thing to H1 is the smallest model on MMLU recall (-6.0 points, CI [-13.0, +1.0], not
significant). Error examples show what a real recall failure under CoT looks like when it happens: the
0.5B model, asked for the term for a tribe with no set homeland, reasoned its way from the obvious
"Nomadic" to "Schematic"; the 14B model talked itself from "Sci/Tech" to "Business" for an article about
Microsoft entering online music. These happen, but at 14B they are balanced by fixes (pattern matching: 7
harmed, 7 fixed; recall: 23 harmed, 40 fixed).

### H2 (CoT adds 10-25 points on logic and arithmetic): direction supported, magnitude not
Arithmetic gains are 2 to 8 times larger than the hypothesised range. This depends heavily on how strict
the direct condition is: with the answer prefilled, models cannot do any visible working, and direct
accuracy on three-operation arithmetic is 0%. A looser "be concise" baseline would shrink the gap.

Logical deduction is in range only at 3B (+13.9). It is negative at 0.5B, below range at 1.5B, and above
range at 7B and 14B.

### H3 (fewer categories benefit as scale grows): not supported, trend is the opposite
More tasks benefit at larger sizes (2 → 4 of 8), all four slopes are non-negative, and harm rates fall
with size in every category. Within this range, larger models are better at using an explicit trace, not
more disrupted by it. ProofWriter makes the point: direct accuracy stays near chance (30-40%) at every
size, while CoT accuracy climbs from 25% to 75%.

### The "internalised reasoning" clause
The data give one pattern that resembles it: on GSM8K the CoT gain peaks at 3B (+75.0) and falls to +65.3
at 14B, because direct accuracy keeps rising (14% → 32%) while CoT accuracy is near its ceiling (89% →
97%). That is larger models doing more of the arithmetic without a trace. But it is a shrinking benefit
caused by a ceiling, not a trace disrupting anything: CoT is still 65 points ahead. Whether the gain keeps
shrinking, or turns negative, in much larger models is untested here.

### What actually goes wrong when CoT hurts
The only clearly negative cell is logical deduction at 0.5B. Of the 142 items the 0.5B model got right
directly and wrong with CoT, 48 were truncated runs: the model restated rule after rule until the token
budget ran out (`results/harm_failure_modes.csv`). The remaining 93 gave a wrong final answer. Since the
0.5B model's direct accuracy on these tasks is at chance (24% and 33%), most of those "correct" direct
answers were lucky guesses, so losing them is not strong evidence of disrupted reasoning. The honest
reading is that a very small model cannot carry out the reasoning, and attempting it costs a few points
through runaway outputs.

### Surprises
- Direct ProofWriter accuracy barely moves with scale (33% → 40%). The 14B model answers "Unknown" for
  about two thirds of items when it cannot reason visibly.
- Fix rate on pattern matching *falls* with size (31% → 13%) while it rises elsewhere; with direct
  accuracy at 91%, the remaining errors are probably mostly label ambiguity that reasoning cannot resolve.

## 6. Limitations

- **One model family, maximum 14B.** The hypothesis is about scale in general; these results cover
  0.5B-14B Qwen2.5 only. Frontier models were not tested, and the claim that very large models are
  disrupted by traces remains open.
- **Not bit-reproducible at the item level.** Batched bfloat16 generation depends slightly on batch
  composition. Re-running 128 items per model in different batches (pilot vs main run) gave identical CoT
  text for only about 25% of items; item-level correctness agreed for 85% (0.5B) and 95% (14B) of CoT
  items and 99% of direct items. Aggregate accuracies should be unaffected in expectation, but the
  bootstrap CIs (which resample items only) understate total uncertainty somewhat, and harm/fix rates
  include this churn. I did not run repeated full passes to quantify it.
- **Strict direct baseline.** Prefilling "Answer:" removes all visible reasoning. This is the cleanest
  contrast but inflates arithmetic gains relative to how people usually prompt.
- **Category assignment is a judgement call.** "Pattern matching" here means text classification, which
  the models do near ceiling (83-96%). Tasks where implicit pattern learning is the point (as in Liu et
  al. 2024) were not included and might behave differently. Ceiling limits how much could be gained on
  SST-2, though not how much could be lost.
- **Token budget.** 1,024 tokens truncated 44% of the 0.5B model's ProofWriter responses; a larger budget
  might change that cell.
- **Contamination.** All public benchmarks may be in training data; this affects both conditions. The
  synthetic arithmetic task is the only contamination-free one.
- **Sample size.** 250-300 items per task gives per-task CIs of roughly ±3 to ±8 points; effects smaller
  than about 3 points per category cannot be resolved. The prompt-robustness check used 100 items per
  task and is incomplete for 14B.
- **Zero-shot only, greedy only, English only.** Few-shot CoT and sampling were not tested.
- **Literature context from memory**, since no review was pre-gathered.

## 7. Conclusions & Next Steps

**Answer.** In Qwen2.5-Instruct from 0.5B to 14B, zero-shot CoT did not reduce accuracy on text
classification or factual recall (effects between -2.2 and +3.8 points, none significantly negative),
improved arithmetic by 48-85 points and logical deduction by up to 29 points, and helped more tasks, not
fewer, as model size grew. The "CoT tax" in this setting is the roughly 75x output-token cost for no gain
on classification, plus a real accuracy loss only for the smallest model on multi-step logic.

**Follow-ups, in order of value:**
1. Repeat on frontier API models (and a second open family) to test whether the GSM8K-style shrinking
   benefit turns into an actual penalty at larger scale.
2. Add tasks designed around implicit pattern learning, where prior work reports real CoT harm.
3. Run 3-5 repeated passes (or sampling with several seeds) to measure run-to-run variance and put proper
   error bars on harm and fix rates.
4. Add a "concise answer, no prefill" baseline to see how much of the arithmetic gap is due to the strict
   direct condition.
5. Raise the token budget for the smallest models to separate "cannot reason" from "ran out of room".

# Planning: The Chain-of-Thought Tax

## Motivation & Novelty Assessment

### Why This Research Matters
Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting is applied almost by default, yet it costs 10-50x more output tokens than
a direct answer. If CoT also *lowers* accuracy on some task types, practitioners are paying twice. Knowing
which task categories benefit, which are taxed, and how that changes with model size lets people route
prompts sensibly instead of using CoT everywhere.

### Gap in Existing Work
No pre-gathered literature review was present in the workspace (the resource-finder stage was skipped), so
this is from my own knowledge of the literature rather than a systematic review:
- Wei et al. 2022 and Kojima et al. 2022 established CoT gains, mostly on arithmetic/symbolic tasks.
- Sprague et al. 2024 ("To CoT or not to CoT?") meta-analysed 100+ papers and found gains concentrated on
  math/symbolic tasks, with near-zero benefit elsewhere - but mostly reports "no gain", not harm.
- Liu et al. 2024 ("Mind Your Step (by Step)") show CoT *hurts* on tasks where verbal deliberation hurts
  humans (implicit pattern learning, etc.).
- Most comparisons mix model families, so "scale" is confounded with training data and recipe.

Missing: a controlled *within-family* scale ladder, with paired item-level statistics, that separates
"CoT gives no gain" from "CoT actively breaks answers the model otherwise gets right".

### Our Novel Contribution
A paired direct-vs-CoT comparison over 4 task categories x 2 datasets each, on five sizes of one model
family (Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B-14B, a 30x parameter range), reporting not only the accuracy delta but the
*harm rate* P(CoT wrong | direct right) and *fix rate* P(CoT right | direct wrong), which are not
distorted by ceiling effects the way raw deltas are.

### Experiment Justification
- Exp 1 (main grid: 5 models x 8 tasks x {direct, CoT}): directly tests the sign and size of the CoT
  effect per category (H1, H2).
- Exp 2 (scale trend): slope of the CoT delta against log2(parameters) per category tests the claimed
  shift of the crossover with scale (H3).
- Exp 3 (prompt-paraphrase robustness: a second CoT wording): greedy decoding has no seed variance, so
  prompt wording is the relevant nuisance variable; checks that conclusions are not one prompt's artefact.
- Exp 4 (error analysis: harm/fix rates, parse failures, truncation, reasoning length): distinguishes a
  real reasoning tax from formatting/extraction artefacts.

## Research Question
Does zero-shot CoT lower accuracy on pattern-matching and factual-recall tasks (by 5-15 points) while
raising it on logical deduction and arithmetic (by 10-25 points), and does the set of categories that
benefit shrink as model scale grows?

## Hypothesis Decomposition
- H1: For pattern matching and factual recall, delta = acc(CoT) - acc(direct) lies in [-15, -5] pp.
- H2: For logical deduction and arithmetic, delta lies in [+10, +25] pp.
- H3: As parameters increase, fewer categories show a positive delta (deltas trend downward with scale).
- (Mechanistic clause "larger models internalise reasoning that traces disrupt" is NOT directly testable
  with behavioural data; at most the results can be consistent or inconsistent with it.)
"5-15%" is read as percentage points of accuracy; relative changes are reported alongside.

Independent variables: prompt condition (direct / CoT), task category, model size.
Dependent variable: item-level correctness (0/1).

## Proposed Methodology

### Approach
The environment has no API key (OPENROUTER_KEY and all provider keys are unset), so API models are not
available. Instead I run real open-weight models locally on the 4x RTX A6000. This is a deviation from the
default instruction, but it gives a cleaner test of the scale claim: one family, one training recipe, five
sizes. Models: Qwen2.5-{0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B}-Instruct (already in the local HF cache; larger sizes are
not cached and the data disk has ~6 GB free, so 14B is the ceiling).

### Tasks (n = 300 items each, fixed seed 42)
| Category | Task | Source | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern matching | AG News topic | fancyzhx/ag_news test | 4 labels |
| Pattern matching | SST-2 sentiment | stanfordnlp/sst2 validation | 2 labels |
| Factual recall | TriviaQA | rc.nocontext validation | free-form, alias match |
| Factual recall | MMLU recall-heavy subjects | cais/mmlu test | 4-way MCQ |
| Logical deduction | BBH logical_deduction_five_objects | lukaemon/bbh (250 items, all used) | 5-way MCQ |
| Logical deduction | ProofWriter OWA depth-5 | tasksource/proofwriter validation | True/False/Unknown |
| Arithmetic | GSM8K | openai/gsm8k test | number |
| Arithmetic | Synthetic multi-step arithmetic | generated here (contamination-free) | number |

### Conditions
- Direct: instruction to give only the answer; the assistant turn is prefilled with "Answer:" and capped
  at 24 new tokens, so the model cannot reason in the output.
- CoT: "think step by step", then a final "Answer: ..." line; 640 new tokens.
- CoT-v2 (robustness): a differently worded reasoning instruction.
Zero-shot, greedy decoding (temperature 0), default system prompt, identical items across conditions.

### Baselines
Direct answering is the baseline for CoT. Chance levels per task are reported (25%, 50%, 20%, 33%, ~0%).

### Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy; paired delta (CoT - direct); harm rate; fix rate; parse-failure and truncation rates; output
tokens (the literal cost side of the "tax").

### Statistical Analysis Plan (fixed before seeing results)
- Per model x task and per model x category: paired bootstrap 95% CI on delta (10,000 resamples of items),
  exact McNemar test, Holm correction across the family of tests at alpha = 0.05.
- H1/H2 are judged per model x category: supported if the point estimate is inside the stated band and the
  CI excludes 0 in the predicted direction.
- H3: OLS slope of category delta on log2(params), CI by item bootstrap; plus the count of categories/tasks
  with a significantly positive delta at each size.
- CoT answers with no parsable final answer count as wrong (reported separately, with a sensitivity
  analysis that excludes them).

## Expected Outcomes
Support: negative deltas on classification/recall, positive on logic/arithmetic, positive deltas shrinking
with size. Refutation: deltas near zero or positive on recall/pattern tasks; no monotone scale trend.

## Timeline
Setup and data 30 min; harness + pilot 40 min; full runs ~60 min on 4 GPUs; analysis 40 min; report 30 min.

## Potential Challenges
- Small models ignoring the output format -> track parse failures, sensitivity analysis.
- Ceiling effects on SST-2 -> harm/fix rates.
- Truncated CoT -> 640-token budget, report truncation rate.
- Disk nearly full -> venv symlinked to the uv cache, only small parquet files downloaded.

## Success Criteria
All 5 models x 8 tasks x 2 conditions completed with item-level outputs saved; each sub-hypothesis gets an
explicit supported / not supported verdict with CIs.
