\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We tested whether zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting taxes accuracy on easy task types and helps less as models grow, using five sizes of \qwen on eight tasks.
It does neither in this family.
\CoT did not reduce accuracy on text classification or factual recall (effects between $-2.2$ and $+3.8$ points, none significantly negative), improved arithmetic by 48--85 points against a no-working baseline and logical deduction by up to 29 points, and helped more tasks, not fewer, as size grew.
The tax we measure is the roughly 75$\times$ output-token cost for no gain on classification, plus an accuracy loss for the smallest model on multi-step logic that is not significant after correction and is tied in part to truncated outputs.

The most valuable next step is to repeat the study on frontier models and a second open family, to test whether the shrinking \gsm benefit becomes a penalty at larger scale.
Further steps are to add tasks built around implicit pattern learning, to run repeated passes so that harm and fix rates carry proper error bars, to add a concise no-prefill baseline, and to raise the token budget for the smallest models to separate ``cannot reason'' from ``ran out of room''.
